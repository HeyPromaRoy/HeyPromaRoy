About

Teaching, research, and secure systems.

I am an adjunct lecturer in New York City, teaching introductory programming at John Jay College of Criminal Justice and Applied Linear Algebra at The City College of New York. In the classroom, I use Python-based labs, hands-on exercises, and structured problem solving to help students connect concepts with practical applications.

My recent research examines two areas: privacy-preserving technology for people at risk of intimate partner violence, and the infrastructure barriers that affect AI access for speakers of underserved languages. My technical work also includes applied cryptography, operating-system telemetry, network security, and secure software design.

Before moving into teaching and research, I worked in healthcare credentialing and data operations, where accuracy, documentation, and traceability were central to the job.

Selected research

Research publications and presentations.

My recent coauthored work examines the hidden technical costs that underserved-language communities encounter before an AI model ever produces an answer.

A
arXiv preprintLM4UC @ IJCAI 2026 · Oral + poster

Measuring the Tokenization Premium: A Cost Audit for Underserved Language Communities

Avijit Roy, Proma Roy, Hrishitva Patel · 2026

Introduces the Tokenization Equity Audit (TEA), a reproducible benchmark that evaluates 120 Python-debugging items across Bengali, Hindi, Arabic, Tamil, and Yoruba. The work connects tokenization inefficiency to unequal API cost, effective context capacity, and local deployment overhead — a 128k nominal context window shrinks to roughly 82k English-equivalent tokens for Bengali.

1.56×GPT-4o token premium for Bengali relative to English; Yoruba reaches 2.37×, showing the issue extends beyond script family.

B
arXiv + SSRN preprintILA 2026 · Poster

Structural Silence: When AI Infrastructure Fails Speakers of Underrepresented Languages

Avijit Roy, Proma Roy · 2026

Uses Bengali as a case study to connect four structural barriers: limited web representation, a severe training-token deficit, tokenization overhead, and connectivity exclusion. It argues that dataset scarcity is an infrastructure problem and that offline-first design can be an equity strategy.

<0.5%of global web content is Bengali despite roughly 285 million speakers; the study also documents an illustrative 67:1 English-to-Bengali training-data gap.
